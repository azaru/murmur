## 2. Why revivals are rare although @name posts are frequent
I classified every @name post by the target's state at the moment of the post (departed = after `done`, before a `revive`).
- Over all 10 runs: 165 @name mentions went to agents still present, 8 to departed agents, 1 @all. Almost every mention of a departed agent revived it (the mechanism works), but only ~5% of mentions target a departed agent.
- The 14-45 @name posts per M run are coordination chatter among the working owners (r4: 51 to present agents, 2 to departed). They need no revival.
- Departures happen in the first 1-3 min, when there is not yet any work to hand out; by the time handoffs exist (>=4 min) the helpers have gone and the team simply replaces them.
- Agents treat a departure as final. 23 posts name a departed agent without an `@` (e.g. M r0 1.7 crane: "Finch called done on oxvg, so I'll take over oxvg ... Please avoid edits there"; M r0 4.6 dunlin: "Crane left without an oxvg patch; I'll take over oxvg"). The pattern is "take over the vacated work", not "call the leaver back". These takeovers cause duplicate claims (M r0 1.7-1.9: crane and heron both claim oxvg within 0.2 min).
- Only two posts in 10 runs deliberately use the callback idea: M r0 4.0 wren ("please rejoin") and MT r1 15.0 heron (@all). Neither the word "call back"/"revive"/"wake" appears in any other post. Heron's @all came directly after reading crane's departure notice (heron's messages file, message 247, then post at 262) so the last sentence of the notice is read at least in that case. I cannot show for the others whether it was read; thinking titles give nothing.
- Late in runs most departed agents had said "my part is done"/"nothing left" and the few left working were token-starved or in the budget end-game; many runs end 3-8 min after the last departure (e.g. MT r3: last done 5.6, end 9.3).

## 4. Task assignment in MT
- Use: runs r0-r3 use the list; r4 never adds anything. Counts: tasks added/assigned: r0 13/10, r1 10/6, r2 11/6, r3 9/6, r4 0/0. task_drop 5/5/3/1/0, task_done 4/0/3/0/0, task_take 7/5/5/2/0.
- Who assigns: it is almost always the first-entered agent building a full five-item list at 0.1-0.5 min. r1: wren added 5 and assigned 6 (finch, robin, lark, swift, heron, linnet); r2: wren 4 assigns; r3: wren 4 assigns; r0: lark 5 assigns plus finch 2 and tern 3. This looks like one agent acting as planner for the others, which is against the non-hierarchical design, although the 'assignees' are peers acting in the first minute. The wake path was not needed here.
- Collision: in every run with a list, two agents built a competing full list in the first 0.5 min (r0 wren+lark, r1 finch+wren, r2 wren+finch, r3 wren+finch). The result is duplicate items and conflicting owners (r0: Tengo is task #4 for lark, #9 for swift, #4 for kite).
- Acceptance: assignees mostly resolve the duplicates by `task_drop` with a note ("Swift is implementing duplicate Tengo task #9; dropped to avoid overlapping edits", r0 0.6) or ignore the item. Only 3 items in r0 and 3 in r2 reach `task_done`, and one of the done notes is "Duplicate assignment for the Scriggo methods repository" (r0 4.0 robin). When an agent leaves, murmur auto-drops its items with "X finished without completing it" (r1 3.6 swift x2, r2 5.5 lark and 5.9 swift, r3 2.3 robin). Those orphaned items are picked up late: r1 Tengo #5 stays free from 3.6 to 10.7 (tern).
- Why no revival via task: all 24 assigns went to agents that were present (assignments are made at 0.2-1.9 min, before the first departures at ~1.4+). Assigning to a departed agent never happened, and the auto-drop on departure removes the item before anyone could assign it back.
- MT r4: all 25 tool calls on the list are reads (`tasks`, 0.3-0.9 min: finch, swift, kite, heron, tern, robin). The list was empty; wren's first post (0.1 min) was a board post claiming expr, and the next agents claimed repos by posting (0.4-0.7), so the list never got a first item. Claims by post settled in 1.1 min. MT r4 also has the lowest-expr score (.2) with wren's 3 edits, but with n=1 I draw nothing.

## 6. Spread: M r3 (0.171) vs M r1 (0.639)
- Coverage and builds. M r1 had 4 repos with 1-2 sustained workers each (edits: scriggo 60 by robin+plover, wasmi 40 by swift+crane, expr 37 by wren+crane, tengo 27 by lark+swift). All four compiled and passed 14-60 tests.
- M r3: expr 32 edits (tern 15, plover 14, wren 3), wasmi 50 edits spread across 5 agents (crane 22, linnet 13, swift 12, plover 2, tern 1), tengo 32 by lark alone (the only task that scored, .86), scriggo only 4 edits (robin, done at 6.6 with "core compiler/runtime work ... not complete"), oxvg 7. The run ended at 12.5 min. The final trees of expr (`vm` build-fail), scriggo (3 of 53 tests pass) and wasmi (exit 101 on build) did not compile: several agents edited the same repo in parallel under a hard stop and no one verified the combined state. Wren, the expr claimant, left at 6.9 after 3 edits ("delegated try runtime/parser implementation to teammates"), so expr was edited by two others without an owner. Run speed also matters: r3 hit the cap in 12.5 min vs 15.6 for r1.
- The revival in r3 (dunlin, oxvg helper) did not matter for the score.
